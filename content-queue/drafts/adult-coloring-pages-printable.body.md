## How do you color a mandala so it looks balanced?

Start at the center and work your way outward, choosing your whole palette before you touch pencil to paper. Picking three to five colors up front keeps the finished page cohesive instead of accidentally rainbow, and coloring from the middle out lets your hand rest on blank paper rather than smudging what you have already done. The key move is to honor the symmetry: color matching sections in each ring the same shade, so the pattern's natural mirroring reads as deliberate. Alternate light and dark tones between rings to create contrast and make the design feel layered. There is no wrong palette, but a simple trick is to repeat one accent color at intervals to tie the whole piece together. Work in good light, take breaks, and let the repetition relax you; the goal is the calm of the process, not a gallery-ready result. Slow, matched, center-out coloring almost always looks balanced. If a section feels flat, add a second, darker layer of the same color along one edge to fake a soft shadow, an easy trick that makes even a beginner's page look finished.
