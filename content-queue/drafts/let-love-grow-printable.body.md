## How do you print wall art at home so it looks professional?

Print wall art on the heaviest paper your printer allows, at the highest quality setting, so the piece looks framed-shop crisp instead of homemade. The single biggest difference between "printed at home" and "looks store-bought" is the paper. Plain copy paper is thin, curls, and shows through the glass; instead reach for 60 to 100 lb matte cardstock or presentation paper, which holds color richly and sits flat behind a frame. A pack of heavyweight matte cardstock costs a few dollars and stretches across dozens of prints, so it pays for itself the first time you skip a store-bought poster. In your print dialog, choose "Best" or "High" quality, set the paper type to matte or cardstock, and select "Actual size" rather than "Scale to fit." If your home printer struggles with color, save the PDF to a thumb drive and print it at a pharmacy or office-supply counter for about a dollar a sheet.
