## What size and paper should you use to print nursery wall art at home?

Print nursery wall art at either 8x10 or 11x14 inches, the two sizes that fit the frames most people already own, and use a heavy matte cardstock (60 to 80 lb, about 160 to 216 gsm) for a professional look. Standard printer paper works in a pinch, but it can look thin and let the frame backing show through. For the crispest result, set your printer to "high quality" or "best," choose "borderless" or "fit to page," and turn off any scaling that shrinks the design to the printable area. If you want larger art, most office supply and photo counters will print an 11x14 or 16x20 from your file for a few dollars on nicer stock. Matte finishes photograph better and avoid the glare that glossy prints throw under nursery lamps. Print a quick draft on plain paper first to check the color and placement before you commit your good cardstock.

## How do you hang nursery prints above the crib safely?

Hang nursery art so that nothing heavy or breakable sits directly above where the baby sleeps, keep framed pieces to the sides of the crib or on the opposite wall, never right over the mattress. Once the room is settled, arrange your prints on the floor first to test the layout before you put a single hole in the wall. A simple trio in matching frames reads as calmer than a scattered mix, and a set of plain white or natural-wood frames from any craft store keeps the focus on the words. For pieces near the crib, removable adhesive strips are safer than nails and let you rearrange as the room evolves, and they spare your walls if you rent. Hang art at your standing eye level, not the baby's, since you are the one reading it during those long night feeds. Leave a little breathing room between frames so nothing feels crowded.

## How do you match nursery printables to your theme and colors?

Match nursery printables to your room by pulling one or two colors straight from what is already there, the crib bedding, the rug, or the wall paint, and choosing art that echoes them instead of introducing a whole new palette. A boho nursery leans into terracotta, sage, and cream; a classic nursery stays soft with blush, gray, and gold; a modern room can go bold with black-and-white type. If your printable comes in a neutral design, it will blend with almost anything, which is why simple cream-and-sage or black-on-white quote art is so forgiving. Coordinate, do not match exactly, one repeated accent color across the prints is enough to tie a gallery wall together.
